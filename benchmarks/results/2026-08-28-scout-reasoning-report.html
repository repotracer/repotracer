Scout reasoning / final native-tool study

Use medium reasoning.

Low was faster but brittle on cross-file explanations. High matched medium’s quality at more than twice the median latency. The current default is already right.

Medium beat low on six paired grades, tied on 18, and never lost. Its mean-score advantage over low was 0.25 points; the 95% task-cluster bootstrap interval was +0.083 to +0.458. Medium was slower in all 24 pairs, with a median paired penalty of 20.01 seconds.

High tied medium on all 24 grades. It took 2.38× the arm-level median wall time, used 6.33× the reasoning-output tokens, and cost 1.98× as much on the benchmark’s equivalent usage rates. It was slower in 21 of 24 pairs; the median paired penalty was 76.22 seconds.

Equivalent cost uses the repository benchmark rates: $0.20/M uncached input, $0.02/M cached input, and $1.20/M output including reasoning. It is a usage comparison, not a provider invoice.

02Blind quality review

The reviewer saw opaque labels, task text, answer text, and citations. Arm, reasoning effort, repeat, latency, and usage stayed hidden until all 72 scores were written. The rubric ran from 0 to 4; no answer scored below 3.

01
Low truncated two cross-file answers. One direct-tool answer stopped mid-citation. One subscription answer stopped during process-tree cleanup.
02
Low produced four weak citation claims. One nonexistent arm-relative path, two setup ranges that did not support the claimed behavior, and one single-line self-update citation presented as the whole lifecycle.
03
Medium and high had no manual defects. Both arms scored 4/4 in every repeat. Automated citation validity was 100% in all arms, which shows why syntax-only citation checks cannot replace review of whether a range supports the claim.

The split is tempting: low for exact lookups, medium for cross-file work. Do not ship it from this study. The same eight tasks revealed and measured the split, so a classifier could simply overfit their wording. Keep medium until a preregistered held-out study proves a stable routing rule.

04What could overturn this

  • One repository. Eight tasks and three repeats make the within-study result stable, not universally general.
  • One blind reviewer. The scoring has no inter-rater calibration. The six low-arm deductions are concrete, but the exact size of the quality gap remains reviewer-dependent.
  • One provider path. This selects reasoning for Luna through the production Codex subscription app-server, not every Luna host or future model revision.
  • Equivalent cost. Usage was complete, but the dollar figures apply benchmark rates rather than an invoice. Cost was secondary by protocol.

Confidence: moderate. High is clearly wasteful within this matrix. Medium is the safer production setting than low. Cross-repository and hybrid-routing claims need new data.

05Method and artifacts

Every arm used the same preserved source snapshot, retained adaptive Scout prompt, model, authentication path, and native Read/Glob/Grep tools. Only reasoning_effort changed. Runs were randomized and executed with four workers.
