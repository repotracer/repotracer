Major iteration / Scout service tier

Fast was faster. We still keep default.

The 48-run pilot cleared every measured quality and latency gate. It did not establish the treatment: all 155 priority-requested Luna model calls reported the default tier.

Decision

Keep Luna medium on the default service tier for the final evaluation. The protocol says ties stay on default, and a treatment that never reports a distinct actual tier is a tie.

Result

Eight repository-search tasks ran three matched repeats per tier. All 48 Scout runs completed. Fast was quicker in 19 of 24 pairs; the paired median improvement was 7.06 seconds. It also preserved 100% citation validity.

Quality held

The arm-blind reviewer scored fast higher in six matched pairs, default higher in two, and tied sixteen. Both arms had one 2/4 result. Each low result omitted crates/core/src/config.rs while tracing the subscription app-server flow; this was a shared task-boundary miss, not a new fast-tier failure.

One grader request exceeded its 1,800-second infrastructure cap after the other 47 grades completed. Retrying that one opaque case succeeded in 17.43 seconds. The first partial F046 trajectory was overwritten before it was separately retained; this report keeps that limitation explicit.

Limits

  • The treatment request reached the provider as priority, but successful model responses reported actual_service_tier=default.
  • Eight scoped tasks test Scout search, not complete Sol task economics.
  • Concurrent model calls support exact arm-level provider aggregation, not defensible per-trial provider-log attribution.
  • The final production evaluation therefore uses the default tier and measures routing, complete quality, cost, and time separately.
