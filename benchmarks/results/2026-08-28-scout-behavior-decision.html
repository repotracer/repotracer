03Do not mix the experiments

  • Tool result: CodeGraph was rejected. Scout keeps Read, Glob, and Grep.
  • Prompt result: the three-call restriction was rejected. Native searches may continue until the requested evidence is complete.
  • Reasoning result: the earlier low/medium/high sweep only tested whether reasoning rescued CodeGraph. It did not choose the best reasoning level for the final native-tool Scout.

A concrete example: exact package-version lookups remained equally good and equally fast. Cross-file lifecycle answers stopped omitting configuration, cleanup, or failure boundaries once Scout could make more than three calls.

04What this still does not prove

  • One repository and one human reviewer do not prove the gain generalizes to every codebase.
  • Prompt tokens are subscription usage, not a trustworthy dollar price. No USD claim is made.
  • One MCP-flow task used eight extra calls without a quality gain. The stopping rule still has waste to remove.
  • A better Scout answer does not automatically reduce Sol’s total work. Only the complete workflow can establish total quality, cost, and time.
  • Two canceled harness attempts had stale-source and relative-path errors. They remain retained but are excluded from the corrected matrix.

05Next experiment

Run Luna low, medium, and high reasoning on this exact retained setup: adaptive prompt plus native Read, Glob, and Grep. Select the cheapest and fastest level that preserves the quality gain.

After Scout is stable, test Sol’s routing and then the complete Sol → Scout → Sol workflow. The final MAH-SWE and public benchmark pass will measure whole-task quality, complete subscription usage, and end-to-end time against the 60% cost-reduction objective.
